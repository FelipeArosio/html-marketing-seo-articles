Wine Tasting Glasses: What They Are and What They Are Used For

During a tasting, the glass becomes an analytical tool. Its transparent bowl makes it possible to observe the wine’s colour and clarity, while the space inside allows it to be swirled in a controlled manner. An opening narrower than the central part directs the aromas towards the nose, while the stem keeps the hand away from the wine and limits heat transfer.

An ordinary drinking glass can also be used in a convivial setting, but the assessment will be less consistent. A wide, cylindrical shape, for example, disperses aromas more quickly, while coloured glass hinders visual examination. For this reason, technical tasting glasses are colourless, undecorated and have a regular rim.

Wine Tasting Glasses: Bowl, Stem, Rim and Base

  • Bowl: it should hold a small amount of wine while leaving enough room for swirling.
  • Opening: when narrower than the central part, it helps concentrate the bouquet.
  • Stem: it provides a stable grip without requiring the hand to wrap around the bowl.
  • Rim: it should be even and comfortable while drinking.
  • Base: it supports the glass and reduces the risk of it tipping over.

ISO Wine Tasting Glasses: Meaning and Characteristics

The ISO glass is the tasting glass standardised by the international ISO 3591:1977 standard for the sensory analysis of wine. The standard, confirmed as current following its 2022 review, describes a bowl shaped like an elongated egg, supported by a stem and base. Its opening is narrower than the convex section, helping concentrate the bouquet.

Its main purpose is not to create a visually striking glass, but to reduce differences between one tasting and another. Using identical glasses makes it easier to compare different samples without introducing shapes or volumes that might favour one wine over another. This is why ISO glasses are widely used in courses, examinations, competitions and blind tastings.

ISO or Universal Wine Glass: Which Should You Choose?

The ISO format prioritises consistency, practicality and comparison. A modern universal wine glass is often larger and can provide more room for aromatic development during table service. Anyone attending courses or organising tastings with numerous samples may prefer the ISO standard, while those looking for a single set for the home will generally find a medium-sized universal glass more convenient.

The Best Wine Tasting Glass: What to Look For

There is no absolute winner for every type of wine. The best model depends on the bottle, frequency of use and context. A transparent, stable and well-proportioned glass is more useful than an enormous bowl chosen solely for its visual impact. The glass should allow the wine to be observed without altering its colour, while the rim should guide the sip without feeling uneven.

Total capacity does not correspond to the amount of wine that should be poured. The empty space is needed to swirl the wine and collect its aromas. Lightness and durability must also remain balanced: a slender stem is pleasant to hold, but it should withstand frequent use and washing.

Transparent and Undecorated Wine Tasting Glasses

A clear, colourless material is preferable for assessing colour, reflections and clarity. Engravings, coloured glass and prominent patterns can interfere with observation. Brilliance makes the wine easier to examine, but does not improve its quality: it is a functional characteristic of the vessel.

Tasting Glasses for Red, White and Sparkling Wine

The shape of the glass can be adapted to the wine’s structure without turning the choice into an inflexible rule. A full-bodied red often benefits from a larger bowl, which provides more surface area and room for swirling. A fresh white normally requires a more compact volume, helping prevent the temperature from rising too quickly.

For sparkling wines, a tulip-shaped glass makes it possible to observe the perlage while providing more room for aromas than a very narrow flute. A flute is not necessarily the wrong choice: it remains practical for simple wines and services that prioritise the visual progression of the bubbles. For a more analytical tasting, however, a slightly wider bowl provides more complete aromatic information.

Wine Tasting Glasses for Red Wine

For red wines, it is useful to provide sufficient surface area for contact with the air without pouring too much wine. The 38 cl Small format is suitable for light- or medium-bodied reds, while a larger model may be preferable for very structured wines. The glass does not remove tannins or hardness, but it can make the aromatic profile easier to interpret.

Wine Tasting Glasses for White Wine

A medium-sized bowl concentrates aromas and helps maintain the wine’s temperature. Matured or particularly complex whites may require more space, while fresh and aromatic styles perform well in more compact shapes. Serving wine excessively cold reduces the perception of its aromas.

Sommelier Glasses: What Are They Really Called?

The expression “sommelier glass” does not identify one mandatory product. Common terms include tasting glass, technical glass, universal wine glass and ISO glass. The latter is the standard reference for comparative assessments, but a sommelier may use different shapes according to the wine, service and objective.

In a restaurant, a professional universal glass provides greater versatility, while the ISO standard facilitates comparison during an examination or blind tasting. The word “sommelier” therefore describes professional use rather than an exclusive shape or specific brand.

Wine Tasting Glasses: Serving Quantities and Dimensions

A 38 cl glass should not be filled to the rim. During a tasting, a limited quantity is poured—enough to observe the colour and swirl the wine without spilling it. The empty space collects aromas and makes it possible to tilt the glass during visual examination.

Height and diameter influence stability and aromatic development. A medium-sized format is often the most practical choice for the home: it takes up less space than a large balloon glass and can be used with different wines. In comparative tastings, it is important to pour similar quantities into every glass.

Wine Tasting Glasses for the Home: Which Should You Buy First?

A medium-sized, transparent and easily replaceable universal glass is a sensible place to start. One carefully selected set can be used for white, rosé, light- or medium-bodied red and some fortified wines. Only those who frequently open very structured reds or complex sparkling wines gain a clear advantage from adding more specialised shapes.

Before purchasing, check the height, diameter, capacity, material and washing instructions. Storage space also matters: very tall or wide glasses can be inconvenient for everyday use. Continued availability of the model will make it easier to replace any glasses that break.

Wine Tasting Glasses: Washing and Storage

Traces of detergent, cloth odours or dust can interfere with a tasting. It is important to rinse the glass thoroughly, allow it to dry completely and inspect it against the light. When polishing it, do not twist the bowl and stem forcefully in opposite directions, as this movement increases the risk of breakage.

The glass should be stored in a stable position in an environment free from strong odours. If it has remained in a cupboard for a long time, a quick rinse before service will help prevent unwanted aromas. It should be held by the stem or base to limit fingerprints and heat transfer.

Wine Tasting Glasses: Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overfilling the bowl and removing the space needed to swirl the wine.
  • Continually holding the glass with the hand wrapped around the bowl.
  • Using coloured glass during visual examination.
  • Assuming that every small wine glass is an ISO tasting glass.
  • Choosing a model solely because it is very large or extremely lightweight.
  • Using a D.O.F. tumbler or handled jar as a technical equivalent of a wine glass.
  • Leaving detergent, cloth or cupboard odours inside the glass.

Wine Tasting Glasses: Conclusions

The best wine tasting glasses share several practical characteristics: transparent glass, a well-proportioned bowl, an opening that directs aromas towards the nose, an even rim, a comfortable stem and a stable base. An ISO glass is useful when comparisons must be standardised, while a medium-sized universal glass is more practical for everyday service.

What Is an ISO Wine Tasting Glass?
