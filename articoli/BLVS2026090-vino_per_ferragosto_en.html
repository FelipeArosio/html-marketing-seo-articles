Wine for Ferragosto: Where to Start When Choosing the Right Wine

Menu, Weather and Number of Guests: Three Questions to Ask Before Opening a Bottle

Before ordering or chilling the bottles, it is worth taking a careful look at the menu. A grilled fish menu calls for a more vibrant, mineral white; a meat barbecue needs a red with generous fruit, soft tannins and good structure. If the table features a combination of seafood, vegetables and meat, the most practical solution is to have at least one white and one red ready to serve.

The number of guests also matters. A 750 ml bottle provides approximately five or six tasting glasses. For a long lunch, it is better to alternate wine with water and choose wines that remain enjoyable even with the second glass. Wine for Ferragosto should encourage conviviality, not become heavy or difficult to drink.

Freshness and Drinkability: Why They Matter More Than Power at Ferragosto

In the middle of August, a wine that is too warm, alcoholic or concentrated can quickly tire the palate. Freshness, savouriness and the correct serving temperature therefore become essential criteria. The organic Antonella Corda Vermentino, made in Serdiana from 100% Vermentino grapes, follows this approach: its citrus profile, white-fruit notes, mineral character and fresh palate make it ideal for light dishes and seafood.

When meat reaches the grill, Antonella Corda Cannonau offers a different yet equally summer-friendly option: red and dark fruit, aromatic herbs, Mediterranean scrub, delicate spices and silky tannins. Served slightly chilled, it becomes a more balanced and food-friendly red for Ferragosto.

Antonella Corda Vermentino di Sardegna DOC 2025: the White Wine for the Fresher Side of the Barbecue

When the August 15 lunch begins with seafood starters, savoury canapés, mildly aged cheeses or grilled fish, the white wine should be fresh without being anonymous. When to Choose a White Rather Than a Red During the August 15 Lunch

White wine is the natural choice when the menu includes delicate dishes, quick cooking methods, aromatic herbs or cold preparations. In warm weather, a white served between 8 and 10°C can add pace to the meal without overpowering the ingredients. The important thing is not to chill it excessively: a temperature that is too low will hide its aromas, citrus notes and savouriness.

For a mixed menu, Vermentino can open the service and remain available as vegetables, fish and fresh cheeses arrive. The red can be introduced later, when the dishes become richer and more flavourful.

Wine for Ferragosto: Which Red to Pair with a Meat Barbecue

Antonella Corda Cannonau di Sardegna DOC 2023: a Mediterranean Red for Grilled and Flavourful Dishes

As soon as sausages, burgers, cuts of beef, arrosticini or aged cheeses reach the grill, the wine for Ferragosto can shift up a gear. Antonella Corda Cannonau di Sardegna DOC 2023, an organic wine made in Serdiana from 100% Cannonau grapes, offers a profile that suits meat without becoming excessively heavy.

It has an intense, vibrant ruby colour. The nose reveals cherry, raspberry, blackberry, rose, aromatic herbs, Mediterranean scrub and delicate spices. On the palate, it combines freshness, softness, silky tannins and persistence. This combination allows the wine to stand up to grilled dishes while remaining enjoyable in a summer setting.

Slightly Chilled Red Wine: the Detail That Transforms the Barbecue

A common mistake is serving red wine at the same temperature as the surrounding environment. At Ferragosto, a bottle left outdoors can quickly exceed 25°C and taste more alcoholic, softer and less precise. For this reason, a red such as Cannonau can be brought to around 14–16°C before serving.

This small precaution keeps the fruit livelier and the tannins more controlled. There is no need to turn it into a cold wine: simply protect it from the sun, keep it away from the grill and chill it moderately.

Wine for Ferragosto: Practical Pairings for Fish, Meat and Vegetables

A Quick Guide for Every Barbecue Dish

To make the choice easier, the menu can be considered according to its main ingredients. Not every dish calls for the same wine, but two well-chosen bottles can cover most of the meal.

  • Grilled fish: Vermentino di Sardegna DOC, for its freshness, citrus notes and savouriness.
  • Prawns and shellfish: a fresh white, served not too cold to enhance their natural sweetness.
  • Grilled vegetables: Vermentino when courgettes, aubergines and peppers are the main ingredients; a soft red when the vegetables accompany meat or cheese.
  • Grilled chicken and turkey: a structured white or a lightly chilled red, depending on the marinade and spices.
  • Sausages, burgers and red meat: Cannonau, for its fruit, soft tannins and Mediterranean notes.
  • Aged cheeses: Cannonau or a broader selection of red wines, especially towards the end of the meal.

What to Avoid in Very Hot Weather

During a summer barbecue, it is best to avoid wines served too warm, bottles left in the sun and pairings based solely on power. Heat increases the perception of alcohol and can make even high-quality wines less enjoyable.

It is better to focus on balance: a fresh white to begin, a slightly chilled red for the meat, water always available on the table and glasses that are not overfilled. This allows each wine to fulfil its role and prepare the palate for the next course.

Wine for Ferragosto: How Much Wine to Plan for the August 15 Lunch

Calculating the Number of Bottles Without Overdoing It

For a long and convivial lunch, the quantity should be planned sensibly. A 750 ml bottle generally provides around five or six tasting glasses. This guideline can help when planning a purchase, but it should not encourage excess: Ferragosto meals often last for several hours, and consumption should remain moderate.

The presence of an aperitif, starters, grilled dishes and dessert changes the amount required. If there are many adults and the menu is varied, it is better to divide the bottles between white and red while ensuring that water is always available.

The Ideal Mix of White and Red

With a seafood or vegetarian menu, white wine should make up most of the selection. If the barbecue begins with fish and moves on to meat for the main part of the meal, an even split between Vermentino and Cannonau works well.

For a barbecue based almost entirely on meat, red wines will play a larger role, but it is still worth providing a white for the aperitif and for guests who prefer fresher wines. The practical rule is simple: the wine should follow the dish, not the desire to open the most prestigious bottle.

Wine for Ferragosto: How to Serve and Store Bottles During the Barbecue

Refrigerator, Ice Bucket and Shade: Protecting Wine from the Heat

On an August day, managing the temperature is just as important as choosing the pairing. White wines should be chilled in advance and then kept in an ice bucket or refrigerator between servings. Red wines should not be left near the grill or on a table in direct sunlight.

If the outdoor temperature is high, Cannonau can also spend a few minutes in the refrigerator before being opened. The goal is not to chill it like a white, but to keep it balanced, with clear fruit and less noticeable alcohol.

Glasses and Serving: Simplicity Without Sacrificing Quality

Tulip-shaped wine glasses or practical everyday wine glasses are sufficient for an outdoor table. Avoiding glasses that are too small makes it easier to appreciate the aromas and structure, especially with Cannonau. Pouring smaller amounts and serving more frequently also helps to maintain a more stable temperature.

Adding ice directly to the wine is not recommended if you want to preserve its aromas and body. It is better to chill the bottle beforehand or use an ice bucket, allowing the wine enough time to express itself in the glass.
