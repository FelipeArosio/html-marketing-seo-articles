Sangiovese Wine: What Kind of Wine It Is and How to Recognise It

Sangiovese is a black grape variety used primarily to produce red wines. It does not, however, represent a single style: terroir, yields, grape ripeness, winemaking and ageing all have a clear influence on the result in the glass. For Romagna Sangiovese DOC, the production regulations and the Consorzio describe a ruby-red colour, an aromatic profile that may recall violets and a harmonious, slightly tannic taste. These are useful reference points rather than an identical description of every bottle.

Sangiovese Wine: Colour, Freshness and Tannins in the Glass

In the most immediate versions, red fruit, freshness and noticeable but well-managed tannins create a dynamic drinking experience. As structure increases, so do concentration, persistence and aromatic complexity. The relationship between acidity and tannins helps explain why Sangiovese often accompanies flavoursome dishes. This is not because “red wine with meat” is an absolute rule, but because these components can balance the succulence and intensity of the sauce.

Young Sangiovese or Riserva: What Really Changes?

A young version generally prioritises immediacy and clearly defined fruit, while a Riserva must meet stricter production requirements and undergo longer ageing before release. In the case of the Predappio subzone, the Consorzio distinguishes between the standard and Riserva categories. For consumers, the most useful difference is practical: greater structure does not automatically mean a better wine, but rather one suited to dishes capable of supporting it.

Sangiovese Wine: Where It Is Produced and Why Terroir Matters

Tuscany is the region most immediately associated with Sangiovese, which plays a leading role in appellations such as Chianti Classico, Brunello di Montalcino and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. In Romagna, however, the grape has an equally central identity: Romagna Sangiovese DOC includes several subzones capable of displaying different nuances.

Romagna Sangiovese: The Role of the Subzones

Consorzio Vini di Romagna recognises 16 subzones within Romagna Sangiovese DOC. This subdivision helps interpret the wine more precisely: origin is not merely a geographical detail on the label, but a factor reflected in structure, ripeness, tannins and aromatic profile.

Predappio Sangiovese: Minerality, Tannins and Ageing Potential

Predappio is described by the Consorzio as a subzone capable of producing mineral, long-lived Sangiovese with a vigorous tannic character. Soils and altitudes vary within the area, meaning that there is no single “taste of Predappio”. It nevertheless provides an essential reference for understanding the two Drei Donà wines featured in this guide.

Drei Donà Sangiovese: Two Interpretations of Predappio

Comparing Notturno in Fa Maggiore and Vigna del Pruno offers a practical way to understand how the same grape variety can change through grape selection and ageing. Both are made entirely from Sangiovese and come from the Predappio subzone, but they pursue different stylistic objectives.

Notturno in Fa Maggiore 2023 Sangiovese: Balance and Versatility

Notturno in Fa Maggiore 2023 is a Romagna DOC Sangiovese Predappio made entirely from Sangiovese. Drei Donà selects grapes from the family’s different vineyards, vinifies them separately and then assembles the cuvée. Fermentation takes place in concrete and stainless steel, followed by approximately one year of ageing in large French oak casks and Allier tonneaux. The profile described by Vinoso.shop reveals cherry, sour cherry, small red berries, violet, delicate spices and balsamic notes, with a dry, fresh and harmonious palate.

Vigna del Pruno Riserva 2021 Sangiovese: Greater Structure and Depth

Vigna del Pruno 2021 is the winery’s historic cru, produced entirely from Sangiovese grown in a single 2.98-hectare vineyard in the Predappio subzone. According to the producer, 20% of the wine ages in terracotta amphorae, while the remaining 80% spends approximately 18 months in French oak tonneaux and barriques, followed by further ageing in the bottle. Compared with Notturno, it is the wine to consider when a dish requires greater depth and persistence.

Wine for Tagliatelle with Ragù: Why Consider Sangiovese?

Ragù combines several elements that require balance: meat, fat, long cooking and, in the Bolognese version, a more restrained tomato component than in many red sauces. Egg pasta and often Parmesan must also be considered. When choosing a wine for tagliatelle with ragù, it is therefore advisable to assess the dish’s intensity and succulence before considering the label.

Sangiovese and Ragù: The Role of Freshness and Tannins

The freshness of Sangiovese can make each sip feel less heavy alongside the sauce’s fatty component, while the tannins find a counterpoint in the meat’s succulence. Gambero Rosso has explicitly recommended a Romagna Sangiovese Riserva with tagliatelle al ragù. This is a useful gastronomic reference, but not a formula that should be applied indiscriminately to every recipe.

Wine for Tagliatelle with Ragù: When to Choose Notturno

With a balanced classic ragù that is not excessively concentrated, Notturno offers a profile combining body, freshness and well-integrated tannins. Its greater immediacy compared with a Riserva allows it to accompany the dish without shifting all the attention towards the wine. It is a particularly sensible choice when the aim is to enhance the fresh pasta while keeping the service smooth and enjoyable.

Pairing Wine with Ragù: When to Choose Vigna del Pruno Riserva

If the sauce becomes more intense—for example, through very long cooking, more flavoursome meat, game or a generous quantity of cheese—it may be appropriate to choose a more structured Sangiovese. Vigna del Pruno has greater depth and persistence and can support dishes that might make a more agile red seem lacking in intensity.

Red Wine with Meat-Based Pasta: How to Choose Beyond Tagliatelle with Ragù

Not all meat-based pasta dishes have the same gastronomic weight. A rich lasagne with béchamel and Parmesan, pasta with a white ragù and a first course made with game place different demands on the wine. “Red wine with meat-based pasta” is therefore too broad a category unless the sauce is also considered.

Red Wine with Medium-Bodied Meat-Based Pasta Dishes

Tagliatelle with ragù, stuffed fresh pasta with meat sauce and dishes in which the fatty component remains controlled can work well with a medium-bodied Sangiovese. Freshness, non-aggressive tannins and enough persistence to remain present after each bite are particularly valuable here.

Red Wine with Meat-Based Pasta: When Greater Structure Is Needed

Very rich lasagne, concentrated ragù or pasta dishes with game require greater volume and continuity on the palate. In these cases, a Riserva may provide a more coherent pairing, provided that it does not dominate the dish. The objective is always to balance the food and wine rather than simply choose the most powerful bottle.

Pairing Wine with Ragù: Mistakes to Avoid

The most common mistake is to begin with colour and choose a red simply because the dish contains meat. A delicate ragù can be overwhelmed by a highly extracted wine, while an intense sauce can make an excessively light red seem weak. Tomato, cheese and the amount of fat also affect the final pairing.

  • Assuming that every ragù is the same.
  • Ignoring the weight of egg pasta, béchamel or cheese.
  • Choosing a Riserva simply because it is the more prestigious version.
  • Serving red wine too warm and accentuating its alcohol and softness.
  • Overlooking the overall structure of the dish and focusing solely on the meat.

Sangiovese and Ragù: Why the Recipe Comes Before the Label

Bolognese ragù, white ragù and wild boar ragù all contain meat but do not have the same intensity. Sangiovese offers several possibilities precisely because it can be produced in different styles: the most convincing choice comes from matching the level of the dish rather than assuming that only one correct pairing exists.

Sangiovese and Ragù Pairings: A Quick Guide

A quick way to choose is to consider increasing levels of intensity: a classic ragù primarily requires balance; lasagne and more concentrated sauces call for greater body; dishes containing game can support a more persistent Riserva. Before deciding, always consider the tomato, fat, type of meat, cheese and cooking time.

Sangiovese Wine: Conclusions for Understanding and Pairing It More Effectively

Sangiovese is a grape variety capable of expressing itself in very different styles, ranging from an immediate red to a deeper Riserva. Tuscany and Romagna are essential regions for understanding its identity, while in Predappio its tannic character and ageing potential assume a particularly recognisable form. Notturno and Vigna del Pruno clearly demonstrate two interpretations of the same variety. Frequently Asked Questions About Sangiovese Wine

What Kind of Wine Is Sangiovese?

Sangiovese is a black grape variety used primarily to produce red wines. It can create fresh, medium-bodied wines as well as more structured versions suited to extended ageing. The result depends on the terroir, grape selection, winemaking and ageing, which is why the name “Sangiovese” does not indicate a single style of wine.

In Which Region Is Sangiovese Produced?

Sangiovese is particularly closely associated with Tuscany, where it plays a leading role in several renowned appellations, but it is also central to Emilia-Romagna. Romagna Sangiovese DOC includes 16 subzones, including Predappio. The variety is grown in other Italian regions as well, but Tuscany and Romagna are essential for understanding its best-known expressions.

Is Sangiovese a Prestigious Grape Variety?

“Prestigious” is not a technical classification for a grape variety. It is more accurate to say that Sangiovese has considerable quality potential: it can produce simple, immediate wines as well as structured crus and Riservas with significant ageing potential. Its role in major Tuscan appellations and Romagna subzones such as Predappio demonstrates how much its expression can change according to the terroir and winemaking project.
